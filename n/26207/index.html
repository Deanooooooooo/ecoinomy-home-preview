Best Crypto Indicators: What Eight Years of Bitcoin Data Show

Ecoinomy editorial team, 6 min read
Last Updated: 28 October, 2025

Updated 16.10.2026 · Written and reviewed by the Ecoinomy editorial team · Facts checked against StockCharts ChartSchool, TradingView, eToro's fee page and ESMA's MiCA register; backtest run on Bitstamp BTC/EUR daily prices on 15 October 2026.

The best crypto indicators are a trend filter (the 200-day moving average or the Ichimoku Cloud), RSI for context and OBV for volume. On BTC/EUR daily prices from 2018 to October 2026, trend filters cut the worst fall from 80% to between 59% and 68%, while dip-buying with RSI, Bollinger Bands or Stochastic RSI lost to simply holding.

The one thing that changes the pick is your cost per trade. At eToro's 1% fee per side, only the Ichimoku rule beat buying and holding bitcoin over the full period. At 0.1% per side, four rules did. Fast signals such as MACD crossovers only pay if your trading costs are tiny.

What did eight years of BTC/EUR data say about each indicator?

Trend-following rules did best and buy-the-dip oscillators did worst. We turned each indicator's textbook signal into a simple long-or-cash rule and ran it on Bitstamp's BTC/EUR daily candles from 1 January 2018 to 14 October 2026, starting with €1,000.

Signals were taken on the daily close and traded at the next open; bitcoin went from €11,205 to €72,813. "Round trips" are completed buy-and-sell pairs; "worst fall" is the largest drop from a previous high, at the 1% fee.

What to remember

  • On BTC/EUR from 2018 to October 2026, the Ichimoku cloud rule turned €1,000 into €10,154 after 1% fees, against €6,358 for buying and holding.
  • Every moving-average and Ichimoku rule in our backtest cut bitcoin's worst fall from 80% to between 59% and 68%; from 2022 the cut was from 64% to between 36% and 48%.
  • RSI, Bollinger Bands and Stochastic RSI used as "buy the dip" signals lost to holding in both test periods, although RSI won 71% of its trades and Bollinger Bands 61%.
  • Fees decide fast strategies: MACD made €11,372 at 0.1% per side but €1,320 at 1%, after 119 round trips.
  • RSI's standard setting is 14 periods with 70/30 levels; MACD is 12, 26 and 9; Bollinger Bands are 20 periods and 2 standard deviations (StockCharts ChartSchool).

Technical indicators turn price and volume into a signal you can act on. They sit at the heart of most crypto trading strategies, but none of them works alone, and our numbers show which ones carry their weight on bitcoin. For the broader method of reading charts, support and resistance, see our guide to crypto technical analysis; for the platforms and basics, start at our crypto trading hub.

Five terms used on this page:

Lagging indicator
Built from past prices, such as a moving average. It confirms a trend after it starts and gives fewer false signals.
Oscillator
An indicator that moves within a fixed range, such as RSI from 0 to 100, used to judge whether a move is stretched.
Divergence
Price makes a new high or low but the indicator does not. Traders read it as a sign that the move is losing strength.
Backtest
Running a rule on past data to see how it would have done, including fees.
Drawdown
The fall from a previous peak to a later low, in percent. Our "worst fall" column is the maximum drawdown.

Which crypto indicator does which job?

Use one indicator per job: a moving average or Ichimoku for trend, RSI or MACD for momentum, Bollinger Bands or ATR for volatility, and OBV or VWAP for volume. Stacking two indicators that measure the same thing adds noise, not confirmation.

Are moving averages the best crypto indicator?

A long moving average is the most useful single filter in our backtest, because it keeps you out of most of a bear market. It is not the most profitable rule: on bitcoin, the price-above-200-day rule made money but traded 42 times and lagged buy and hold.

A simple moving average (SMA) is the plain average of the last N closes; an exponential moving average (EMA) weights recent closes more, as StockCharts ChartSchool explains. Common uses:

  • Golden cross: the 50-day SMA crosses above the 200-day SMA, read as a new uptrend.
  • Death cross: the reverse, read as a new downtrend.
  • Trend filter: only take long trades while price is above the 200-day SMA.
  • Dynamic support: pullbacks to the 20- or 50-day EMA in a strong trend.

In our data, the golden cross traded only 10 times in almost nine years and won 60% of them, but it entered late and exited late. The faster 20/50 EMA crossover made more (€5,529 after 1% fees), at the cost of 33 round trips. Moving averages suit trend followers on daily and weekly charts; they whipsaw you in sideways markets.

Does RSI work for crypto trading?

RSI works as context, not as a buy-the-dip trigger on bitcoin. Buying below 30 and selling above 70 won 71% of trades in our test but still turned €1,000 into €843 after fees, because a few losing trades were huge: one bought in May 2022 and lost 39%.

The Relative Strength Index, developed by J. Welles Wilder and published in his 1978 book, oscillates between 0 and 100. Wilder's default is 14 periods, and readings above 70 and below 30 are read as overbought and oversold (StockCharts ChartSchool, RSI). Better uses in crypto:

  • Divergences: price makes a new high while RSI makes a lower high.
  • Filter, not trigger: avoid opening a new long when the daily RSI is already above 70.

RSI suits swing traders and ranging markets. It is not for you if you plan to sell every time it crosses 70: in a strong bitcoin run it can stay there for weeks.

Do Bollinger Bands work on bitcoin?

Bollinger Bands are a good volatility gauge and a poor buy signal on bitcoin. Buying closes below the lower band and selling at the middle band was the worst rule in our backtest: €1,000 became €157 after 1% fees, despite 61% winning trades.

John Bollinger's bands sit 2 standard deviations above and below a 20-day simple moving average (StockCharts ChartSchool, Bollinger Bands). They widen when volatility rises and narrow when it falls. Uses that hold up better than band-touch trading:

  • Squeeze: unusually narrow bands often come before a large move, though not its direction.
  • Walking the band: closes along the upper band show a strong trend, not a sell signal.
  • Breakout confirmation: a close outside the bands on rising volume.

The trap shows in the data: the rule bought falling knives, such as November 2018 (−35%) and February 2020 (−27%). Many small wins did not cover a few large losses.

Ichimoku Cloud: the strongest rule in our test

The Ichimoku Cloud gave the best result in our test. Holding bitcoin only while it closed above the cloud turned €1,000 into €10,154 after 1% fees (€17,162 at 0.1%), against €6,358 for holding, with a worst fall of 59% instead of 80%.

The indicator was developed by journalist Goichi Hosoda and published in 1969. Its default settings are 9, 26 and 52 periods, and the cloud is plotted 26 periods ahead (StockCharts ChartSchool, Ichimoku Cloud). Its five lines:

  • Conversion Line: midpoint of the 9-period high and low.
  • Base Line: midpoint of the 26-period high and low.
  • Leading Span A and B: together they form the cloud; Span B uses the 52-period range.
  • Lagging Span: the close plotted 26 periods back.

The catch: it won only 34% of its 29 round trips. The profits came from staying in long trends, so you need the patience to sit through many small losses. It is not for you if you trade intraday or dislike a crowded chart.

Is Stochastic RSI better than RSI?

Stochastic RSI is faster than RSI, not better. It gives many more signals, and on bitcoin that cost money: its crossover rule made 66 round trips and turned €1,000 into €522 after 1% fees, against €843 for plain RSI.

Tushar Chande and Stanley Kroll built StochRSI to measure where RSI sits within its own high-low range, from 0 to 1, because RSI can stay between 80 and 20 for long stretches without reaching extremes (StockCharts ChartSchool, StochRSI). A move above 0.80 counts as overbought and below 0.20 as oversold (80 and 20 on charts that scale it to 100). It suits short-term traders on low fees who use it to time entries within a trend that another indicator has already confirmed.

What does OBV add to a crypto chart?

OBV tells you whether volume is backing the move. Rising price with falling OBV is a warning; on its own, though, an OBV rule traded 132 times in our test and only paid at very low fees.

On-Balance Volume was developed by Joe Granville, who explained it in a 1963 book; it adds a day's volume when the close is up and subtracts it when the close is down (StockCharts ChartSchool, OBV). Use it for confirmation:

  • A breakout with OBV at a new high has real buying behind it.
  • A new price high with a lower OBV high is a bearish divergence.

One limit is specific to crypto: bitcoin trades on many exchanges at once, so the volume on your chart is one exchange's share, not the whole market. Our test used Bitstamp's volume only.

When is VWAP useful in a 24/7 market?

VWAP is an intraday tool. It shows the average price paid during the current session, weighted by volume, so day traders use it as a fair-value line: buyers in control above it, sellers below it.

StockCharts ChartSchool defines VWAP from intraday data for a single market day, from the open to the close. Crypto never closes, so each chart picks its own reset time; check which one yours uses before comparing levels with other traders. We did not include VWAP in the daily backtest because it resets every session. It is not for you if you hold positions for weeks; use a moving average instead.

Most "best indicator" lists add MACD, ADX, Aroon, CCI, Chaikin Money Flow, Parabolic SAR, SuperTrend, Fibonacci levels and volume profile. Each does a job one of the core seven already covers, so add one only when it answers a question your chart cannot.

What is the best crypto volume indicator?

For most traders, OBV is the best crypto volume indicator on daily charts and VWAP is the best one intraday. Volume profile is the strongest for finding support and resistance, because it shows the price levels where most trading happened.

Whichever you choose, use it to confirm a price signal rather than as a signal of its own. In our test, an OBV-only rule made 132 round trips and turned €1,000 into €899 after 1% fees. Our guide to crypto trading volume explains how to read volume bars, spikes and thin markets.

How do you build custom indicators for crypto trading?

The usual route is TradingView's Pine Script, a language for writing your own indicators and strategies and backtesting them on TradingView's servers. Most of TradingView's built-in indicators are written in it, and users have published more than 150,000 community scripts (Pine Script v6 docs).

A custom indicator is often just a standard one with your own settings or a combination of two. This Pine Script v6 example draws the 200-day trend filter from our test and colours the background green or red:

//@version=6
indicator("200-day trend filter", overlay=true)
len = input.int(200, "SMA length")
ma = ta.sma(close, len)
plot(ma, "SMA", color = close > ma ? color.green : color.red)
bgcolor(close > ma ? color.new(color.green, 90) : color.new(color.red, 90))

On TradingView's pricing page, the free plan allows 2 indicators per chart and the top paid plan 50 (checked 15 October 2026). Two is enough for a trend filter plus one momentum or volume check. Not for you if you will not test the script: a custom indicator that has never been backtested is only an opinion drawn on a chart.

Which indicators suit crypto day trading and futures?

For day trading, VWAP plus a fast EMA (9 or 20 periods) and RSI for momentum is the common set. For futures, the indicators are the same as for spot; what changes is leverage, which makes a wrong signal far more expensive.

Short timeframes multiply the fee problem in our table. A rule that makes 100 round trips a year at 1% per side pays about twice its stake in fees every year, which no indicator edge covers. Day trading only adds up on very low per-trade costs.

Futures traders also watch data that spot charts do not have, such as funding rates and open interest. At EU brokers, leveraged crypto exposure is often a CFD: eToro's fee page, linked above, says its short and leveraged crypto positions are executed as CFDs. Creating trading strategies with indicators

A strategy is a written set of rules: which indicator decides the trend, which one times the entry, where the stop goes and how much you risk. Turn the toolkit above into rules in this order:

  1. Pick one indicator per job. Trend (200-day SMA or Ichimoku), momentum (RSI or MACD), volatility (Bollinger Bands or ATR, Wilder's volatility measure), volume (OBV or VWAP).
  2. Write the entry rule. Example: buy when price closes above the 200-day SMA and the daily RSI is below 70.
  3. Write the exit rule. Example: sell on a daily close below the 200-day SMA.
  4. Size the position. Example: risk 1% of your capital per trade, with the stop below the last swing low or 2 ATR from your entry.
  5. Count the fees. Multiply your round trips per year by twice your fee per side before you start.
  6. Keep records. Track win rate, average win and loss, and worst fall, and review them every month.

The example rule in steps 2 and 3 is the "200-day SMA plus RSI below 70" variant we also backtested: from 2022 it turned €1,000 into €1,786 after 1% fees, with a 45% worst fall against 64% for holding. When you buy matters as much as what you watch; see our guide to crypto market timing.

How should you backtest a crypto indicator before using it?

Test the rule on several years that include a bull market, a bear market and a sideways stretch, with your real fee per trade, and judge it by its worst fall as well as its final return. A rule that only works in one window is curve-fitted.

  • Use the fee you will actually pay. MACD in our test went from €11,372 at 0.1% to €1,320 at 1%.
  • Check more than one start date. Our 2018 and 2022 runs ranked several rules differently.
  • Look at the worst fall. Ask whether you would really have held through it.
  • Do not trust the win rate. Bollinger Bands won 61% of trades and still lost 84%.
  • Keep data back. Tune settings on one period, then test them on a later one you did not touch.

Paper trading is the next step after a backtest; our paper trading guide shows how to run a rule live without real money.

Pitfalls and limits of crypto trading indicators

Indicators describe what price and volume already did. They cannot see news, hacks, regulation or a large holder selling, and every one of them gives false signals.

Indicators do not win every trade

Even the best rule in our test, the Ichimoku cloud, lost on two out of three trades. What matters is the size of the wins against the losses over many trades.

Too many indicators on one chart

Five oscillators that all measure momentum will agree with each other and still be wrong together. One per job is enough.

Overtrading

Acting on every signal multiplies fees. The two most active rules in our table, MACD and OBV, made 119 and 132 round trips and lost most of their gross gains to 1% fees.

Curve-fitting the settings

Tuning a setting until the backtest looks perfect fits the past, not the future. We used each indicator's standard settings for that reason.

Ignoring the custody and licence question

An indicator signal is only as good as the platform you act on. Since MiCA's transition periods ended, check that your exchange or broker appears in ESMA's register; our MiCA licence tracker lists authorised firms by country.

Questions readers ask about crypto indicators

What is the best indicator for crypto trading?

No single indicator is best on its own. In our BTC/EUR test from 2018 to October 2026, the Ichimoku cloud and the 200-day moving average worked best as trend filters, cutting the worst fall from 80% to about 60%. Pair one of them with RSI for momentum and OBV for volume, and judge any rule after your real trading fees.

What are the best indicators for crypto day trading?

Day traders usually combine VWAP as an intraday fair-value line, a fast 9- or 20-period EMA for direction and RSI or Stochastic RSI for momentum. Fees matter even more here: a rule that makes 100 round trips a year at 1% per side pays about twice its stake in fees every year, so day trading needs very low per-trade costs.

What is the best crypto volume indicator?

OBV is the most useful volume indicator on daily charts, VWAP is the standard intraday, and volume profile is best for finding support and resistance. Use volume to confirm a price signal, not as a signal by itself: in our test an OBV-only rule traded 132 times and lost money after 1% fees.

Can you create custom indicators for crypto trading?

Yes. TradingView's Pine Script lets you write your own indicators and strategies and backtest them on its servers, and more than 150,000 community scripts have been published. TradingView's free plan allows 2 indicators per chart, checked on 15 October 2026. Backtest any custom indicator with your real fees before trading it.

What is the best indicator for crypto futures trading?

Futures use the same indicators as spot: a trend filter, a momentum oscillator and a volume check. Futures traders add funding rates and open interest, which spot charts do not show. Leverage makes every false signal more expensive, so use wider stops and smaller positions than you would on spot.

Is RSI a good indicator for crypto?

RSI is good for context and divergences but weak as a standalone buy signal on bitcoin. Buying below 30 and selling above 70 won 71% of trades from 2018 to October 2026, yet turned €1,000 into €843 after 1% fees, because a few losing trades were very large. RSI can stay above 70 for weeks in a bull run.

How many indicators should you use on one chart?

Two to four, each doing a different job: one for trend, one for momentum and optionally one each for volatility and volume. More indicators that measure the same thing, such as RSI, Stochastic and CCI together, add noise rather than confirmation and tempt you into overtrading.
